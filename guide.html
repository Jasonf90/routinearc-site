How do I…

Short walkthroughs for the things people actually want to do.

Capture something by typing

The quickest way in. Type the way you would say it and RoutineArc works out whether it is a task, an event or a note, and pulls out the date, time, place and priority.

  1. Tap the "Add task, event, or note..." bar at the top of Today, Tasks, Calendar, Notes or Events.
  2. Type naturally — "dentist tomorrow at 3pm", "lunch with Sarah at Cafe Roma thursday noon", "p2 renew the passport".
  3. Press send. The item is filed straight away.

Several things at once works: "call mom and buy milk" becomes two items. "buy milk and eggs" stays one, because that is one errand.

If part of what you typed cannot be parsed, that part is kept as a plain task in your own words rather than dropped.

Capture by speaking

Dictate instead of typing. Speech is turned into text on your device, or in the cloud only if you agree.

  1. Tap the round + button at the bottom right of Today.
  2. Choose "Voice dictate", tap the mic, then speak.
  3. Tap "Stop". Check the items it produced, edit anything that is wrong, then Save all.

Nothing is saved until you confirm, so a misheard word costs you a tap, not a wrong entry.

If your device cannot handle your language on-device, RoutineArc asks whether to use Apple's cloud recognition instead. Say no and type it — nothing is sent unless you agree.

Capture from a photo or screenshot

Point the camera at an appointment card, a flyer or a whiteboard, or pick a screenshot from your library, and RoutineArc reads the text off it.

  1. Tap the + button on Today, then "Scan text (OCR)".
  2. Choose Camera or Library.
  3. Review what it found and save.

Text recognition runs entirely on your device — the picture is never uploaded.

A photo of a card stays one item. A photo of a bulleted list becomes one item per bullet.

If the photo has no readable text, RoutineArc says so rather than closing silently.

Start a task from a picture

Keeps the picture itself rather than reading it — the shelf you need to match, the form you have to fill in, the parking bay you left the car in.

  1. Tap the + button on Today, then "Attach photo to task".
  2. Choose Camera or Library.
  3. The task opens with the picture already attached; give it a title and save.

Different from "Scan text (OCR)", which pulls the words out and throws the picture away — this one keeps the picture.

Plan my day

Turns your events and open tasks into an order of work, fitting tasks around fixed commitments and leaving room for travel.

  1. On Today, scroll down to the Day Plan section. "Export to PDF" and "Plan my day" sit beside its heading.
  2. Tap "Plan my day".
  3. Tap "Export to PDF" if you want it on paper.

A task with "Work Task" turned on in its edit form is only scheduled inside the hours in your work schedule.

Set priorities and pick the one that matters

RoutineArc uses the Eisenhower matrix: urgent and important, in four levels from P1 to P4.

  1. Open a task (tap "Edit" first, unless it opened ready to edit) and tap a priority, or say it when you capture ("p2 renew the passport").
  2. Turn on "Eat That Frog" to mark the single most important task of the day; it pins to the top of Today.
  3. On the Tasks tab, switch to the matrix view to see all four quadrants and drag a task between them.
  4. Inside a task, "Urgent" and "Important" are separate switches under EISENHOWER QUADRANT. Setting the P number moves both switches; moving the switches does NOT change the P number.
  5. The "On Deck" card in Today's Priorities section is whichever task the app thinks is next. Tap it to open the On Deck screen and work through them one at a time — that card is the only way in, so keep the Priorities section shown if you use it.

Priority and quadrant are the same fact shown two ways, but only priority drives the pair. To change both at once, drag the task between quadrants in the matrix view on the Tasks tab.

Only one task can be the frog at a time. Marking a second moves the badge.

Make something repeat

Tasks and events can repeat daily, weekly, monthly, quarterly, or on a custom interval.

  1. Open the task or event (tap "Edit" first, unless it opened ready to edit) and choose how often under REPEAT.
  2. For weekly, pick which days. For monthly, pick the day of the month.
  3. For anything else, choose Custom, type a number beside "Every" and pick days, weeks or months.

Work in a focus session

Three timers. When you start focus from a task, the time you actually spend is recorded so Insights can show it back to you. A run on the Focus tab without a task is not recorded.

  1. Open the Focus tab and pick Pomodoro, Timer or Stopwatch.
  2. Or start focus straight from a task, which preloads a sensible length.
  3. In Pomodoro, turn on Strict Mode if you want leaving the app to fail the run. It is not offered on the plain timer or the stopwatch.
  4. In Pomodoro, turn on Ambient Sound for background noise while it runs.
  5. On the stopwatch, each lap collects under Lap History.

Clear the backlog: triage, weekly review, Keep It Honest, On Deck

Four different ways of dealing with what did not get done, each suited to a different mood.

  1. Evening Triage, for tasks whose due date has already passed: swipe RIGHT for Rollover, which brings it forward to today, DOWN to Complete it, LEFT to Delete it.
  2. Weekly Review, for the wider backlog: swipe RIGHT to schedule it, LEFT to leave it alone, DOWN to delete it. The directions are NOT the same as Triage — see the note below.
  3. Keep It Honest: for tasks that keep slipping. Each one must be done, deferred, delegated or deleted.
  4. On Deck: one task at a time, with a timer, when everything feels like too much.
  5. Both decks show buttons on every card as well, so you never have to swipe if you would rather tap.

THE TWO DECKS DISAGREE. In Triage, swiping left deletes; in Weekly Review it only leaves the task alone. Swiping down completes in Triage but deletes in Weekly Review. Read the label on the card before you fling it — a habit learned in one deck will destroy a task in the other.

Triage and Weekly Review are prompted at times you choose under Settings → Planner → Reviews.

Save places and get leave-by times

Store the places you go often and RoutineArc works out when you need to set off.

  1. Turn on Settings → AI → Address lookup → "Look up addresses" first.
  2. Go to More → Saved Places and add a place, with an arrival buffer if you like to be early.
  3. Put that place on an event and Today will show when to leave.

Address lookup is off by default and uses a network lookup, which is why it is opt-in.

Tell it when you work

Your shifts shape the plan: work tasks are scheduled inside them, and the calendar shows them as bands behind your events.

  1. Go to Settings → AI → Data & Memory → Work Schedule. Once you have a shift, tapping it on the Calendar and then "Edit work schedule" brings you back here.
  2. Add a shift with its date, times and location.
  3. Set it to repeat, and how many months ahead to project it.

Manage what RoutineArc remembers

RoutineArc picks up facts about you — where the gym is, that you prefer mornings — and uses them when it files new things.

  1. Open Settings → AI → Data & Memory → Memory to read, edit or delete anything it knows.
  2. Use "Tell it something" to add a fact yourself.
  3. Turn "Learn facts automatically" off in Settings → AI → AI Features if you would rather confirm each one: a card on Today asks before anything is saved.

Memory is stored only on your device, and you can delete any fact at any time. With address lookup on, an address it fills into a task or event is looked up like one you type.

Choose the AI backend

Two options, and both run entirely on your device.

  1. Open Settings → AI → AI Backend.
  2. Apple Intelligence, where your iPhone or iPad has it, is built into iOS and runs on the Neural Engine, so it uses little battery and needs no download. This is the recommended choice.
  3. Rules engine needs no model at all and always works, but is far less flexible.

If Apple Intelligence is off or still preparing, RoutineArc runs the rules engine meanwhile and switches over when it becomes available.

Turn on reminders and the iPhone Live Activity

Reminders are off until you ask for them.

  1. Open Settings → Alerts → Notifications.
  2. Turn on "Local Reminders".
  3. Optionally turn on "Live Activity" on iPhone, which puts the current block on the Lock Screen and, where the iPhone has one, the Dynamic Island.
  4. Under Sounds & Vibration, set sound and vibration separately for timers and reminders.

If reminders ever stop being scheduled — usually because notification permission was revoked — RoutineArc tells you once rather than failing quietly.

Put RoutineArc on your home screen

Five widgets: Home, Tasks, Priority Matrix, Day Briefing and Day Overview.

  1. Turn on Settings → Alerts → Notifications → "Background widget updates".
  2. Touch and hold the Home Screen, tap Edit → Add Widget, and find RoutineArc.
  3. Tap a task in the widget to complete it, or + to capture something.

Widget updates are off by default because they cost a little battery.

Try RoutineArc, then unlock it

RoutineArc is free to use for seven days. After that it keeps everything you already have and stops accepting new items, and editing existing ones until you unlock it.

  1. Use it normally for the first seven days — nothing to sign up for, and no card. Automatic daily backups come with monthly, yearly and the lifetime unlock; backing up by hand, restoring, sharing and deleting are free for good.
  2. Three ways in, whenever you like: the row at the top of Today, the first row of More, or the RoutineArc Unlimited card at the top of Settings → System.
  3. Four ways to pay: a monthly or yearly subscription, the one-time Essentials unlock, or the one-time lifetime unlock. Monthly, yearly and lifetime also turn on the automatic daily backup; Essentials does not.
  4. On a new iPhone or iPad, tap Restore purchases — the unlock follows your Apple Account, not the device.
  5. When the week ends, RoutineArc explains what changed once, and never asks again.

Read-only does not mean locked out. Everything stays readable, searchable and exportable, you can still tick tasks and events off, defer what you cannot get to, and delete whatever you like.

There is no account. The purchase is between you and the App Store; RoutineArc only ever learns whether this Apple Account holds an unlock.

Back up, restore and import

Everything is stored on your device, so backups are yours to keep.

  1. Settings → System → Backup & Restore → Export data writes a JSON file and offers the share sheet.
  2. In the same card, turn on "Automatic daily backup" (it comes with the monthly, yearly or lifetime unlock, not the free week or Essentials), and restore or delete any of them from the list.
  3. The Batch Imports & Templates card downloads CSV templates for tasks, events, work schedule and rhythms, and imports a filled-in CSV.

Restoring replaces tasks, events, notes, the work schedule and rhythm profiles. It is not a merge.

Change what you see

Both the Today screen and the bottom tab bar are yours to arrange.

  1. Tap Customize on Today to reorder or hide sections (up to four at once).
  2. On the same screen, choose which tabs appear, up to five.
  3. Under Settings → Style, the Appearance card sets light or dark, Color Palette the colours, and Layout a compact layout.

Notes, Events and Insights are hidden by default — turn them on here, or reach them from More.

Every screen

One entry per screen, for when you want to know what a control does.

Today

The screen the app opens on. Top to bottom: how long is left of your free week, the date and how many tasks you have finished, the capture bar, a Memory card when it has something to ask or undo, your priorities, today's events, and the day plan.

  1. Type into the bar that says "Add task, event, or note..." to capture anything. It works out whether you meant a task, an event or a note.
  2. Under Priorities, the "On Deck" card is the single thing to do next. Tap it to open the On Deck screen, where you work through them one at a time.
  3. A task marked TODAY'S MIT is the one whose “Eat That Frog” switch you turned on in a task's edit form; it stays pinned to the top until it is done.
  4. Tap the circle to the left of any task to complete it. Tap the row itself to open it.
  5. Tap "Plan my day" to build a schedule around the events you already have.
  6. When the plan says nothing is due today and counts your backlog, tap "Fit backlog into today" to fit your undated tasks into the gaps between events.
  7. Tap "Export to PDF" to share the plan, and "Customize" to choose which of these sections appear at all.
  8. Tap the gear, top right, for Settings.

Most of this screen is optional: "Customize" can hide any section you do not use. The day plan is the exception — it is pinned, and shows a padlock there rather than a switch. The Memory card is not a section: it shows only while it has something to ask or undo.

The row at the very top shows the trial countdown while it is running; the unlock card in Settings → System shows the same count.

Tasks

Every task you have, as a list or as the Eisenhower matrix. The list can be ordered by priority or grouped by tag.

  1. Use "Search tasks…" to find something by title.
  2. The first row of chips filters by area — All, Work or Personal.
  3. The second row filters by state — Active, P1, P2, P3, P4 or Done.
  4. Tap the tag button, top right, to switch between priority order and grouping by tag. In both, the P number orders them first; between two tasks of the same priority, overdue comes before dated, and undated goes last.
  5. Top right: a checkbox to start selecting tasks, a toggle between the list and the four-quadrant matrix, the sort toggle, and + to add.
  6. In the matrix, drag a task between quadrants to change whether it is urgent, important, both or neither.
  7. To export, tap the checkbox to start selecting, choose the tasks you want, then tap "Share PDF". The export is what you selected, not what the filters show.

The filters combine. "Work" plus "P1" shows only urgent, important work tasks.

The matrix is the fastest way to re-prioritise several tasks at once, because moving a card sets both flags in one gesture.

Calendar

Your events on a timeline, by day or by week, with your working hours shaded in.

  1. Use "Day" and "Week" at the top to change the span.
  2. Tap the arrows either side of the date to move backwards and forwards.
  3. Tap + at the bottom right to create one, or tap any block to open it.
  4. To change your work hours, tap a work shift — the thin bar at the edge of its shading, or its name under the date in the day view — then "Edit work schedule". With no shift yet, use Settings → AI → Data & Memory → Work Schedule.

A red line marks the current time on the day view.

Events with a place show it under the title. Opening one gives you "Navigate", which opens the place in Maps.

Events

A plain list of every event, and the screen for editing one.

  1. Tap an event to open it, then "Edit" to change anything (on an iPad, the Calendar’s two-pane day view opens it ready to edit).
  2. Set "Start" and the end time, or turn on "All day" to drop the times entirely.
  3. Under REMINDER, choose how long before the event you want to be told.
  4. Under REPEAT, make it recur — daily, weekly, monthly, or a custom interval.
  5. Under LOCATION, type a place or pick a saved one. Once it is saved, "Navigate" opens the place in Maps whenever you open the event again.
  6. Tap "Save" to keep the changes, or "Delete Event" to remove it.

Notes

Anything that is not a task or an event — with a summary when a note gets long.

  1. Tap + at the top right to write one, or tap a note to open it.
  2. Use "Search notes…" to find one by title or content.
  3. Touch and hold a note and tap "Summarize" ("AI Summarize" while Apple Intelligence is on) to condense it and pull out its action items. Tap "Done" to close the summary.
  4. Tap "Save" to keep it. The X closes the editor without saving, so save before you leave.

Focus

Three timers — Pomodoro, a plain countdown, and a stopwatch — with a history of what you finished.

  1. Pick "Pomodoro", "Timer" or "Stopwatch" at the top.
  2. For Pomodoro, set "Focus (mins)" and "Break (mins)" under Pomodoro Settings.
  3. Press the play button to start. The buttons either side reset and skip.
  4. Under "Pomodoro" only: "Strict Mode" fails the run if you leave the app, and "Ambient Sound" plays background noise. Neither appears on "Timer" or "Stopwatch".
  5. On the stopwatch, laps collect under "Lap History".

Focus time is recorded only when focus starts from a task — the "Start Focus" button on any task, or its Focus chip — which ties the session to that task, so the time shows up in Insights against it. A run on the Focus tab without a task is not recorded.

Insights

What actually happened: focused time, how good your estimates were, the spread across Eisenhower quadrants, and a 60-day completion heat map.

  1. Open More, then Insights & Analytics.
  2. Read the estimate accuracy figure to see whether you routinely under-estimate work.
  3. Use the quadrant spread to check you are not living entirely in urgent-and-important.
  4. The heat map shows which days you completed anything at all.

Everything here is computed on the device from your own history. Nothing is uploaded, and there is nothing to switch on.

More

The directory of everything that is not a tab.

  1. The first row is RoutineArc Unlimited — the monthly and yearly subscriptions, the Essentials unlock and the lifetime unlock.
  2. Workspace holds Habits, Insights & Analytics, Saved Places, All Events, Notes & Ideas, Evening Triage and Weekly Review.
  3. System holds the "Aggressive Memory" switch, Settings, this guide and the privacy policy.

Task detail

Everything about one task. It is the longest screen in the app, so it is grouped into labelled sections you can skip past.

  1. On iPhone, open a task and tap "Edit" to change it; a new task, and a task opened in the Tasks pane on an iPad, open ready to edit.
  2. PRIORITY sets P1 to P4, and moves the "Urgent" and "Important" switches under EISENHOWER QUADRANT to match. Those switches can be set on their own, but doing so leaves the P number where it was.
  3. Turn on "Eat That Frog" to pin this task to the top of today as your most important one.
  4. COMPLETE BY sets the due date. Turn on "Include Time" if the hour matters; leave it off for a whole day.
  5. ENERGY & FOCUS records how long you think it will take and how much energy it needs, which is what "Plan my day" on Today schedules around.
  6. REPEAT makes it recur. Choose a preset, or "Every" a custom number of days, weeks or months.
  7. LOCATION takes a place. Type one into "Add location", tap the crosshair beside it to use where you are now, or tap one of your saved places under it ("Manage" edits that list). Once it is saved, "Navigate" opens the place in Maps whenever you open the task again.
  8. SUBTASKS breaks the job up. Type into "Add subtask...", or tap "Suggest" beside SUBTASKS ("AI Suggest" while Apple Intelligence is on) to have them drafted for you.
  9. CUSTOM TAGS, DELEGATED TO and PICTURE ATTACHMENT cover the rest: your own labels, who owns it, and a photo.
  10. Tap "Save" to keep changes, "Start Focus" to work on it now, or "Delete Task" to remove it.

Setting a due date with a time, on a task with a location, is what produces a leave-by warning.

Tap "Suggest" beside PRIORITY to have the title and notes read and a priority picked on the form. Nothing is saved until you tap "Save".

Nothing on this screen is required. A task with only a title is perfectly valid.

Settings

Six tabs, reached from the gear on Today. The tab strip wraps onto a second row when it needs to, so every tab is visible without scrolling sideways. On each tab the settings are grouped into cards, and a card opens when you tap its heading; only the unlock card starts open.

  1. AI: "AI Backend" picks what reads what you type, and "AI Features" switches individual features on and off. "Data & Memory" leads to "Memory", "Saved Places" and "Work Schedule", and clears focus history. "Address lookup" holds "Look up addresses", for network geocoding.
  2. Planner: "Day Window" sets "Day starts" and "Day ends", "Business Rhythm" picks a rhythm, and "Reviews" chooses when the "Morning Review" and "Evening Review" prompts appear.
  3. Alerts: "Notifications" holds "Local Reminders", "Background widget updates" and "Live Activity" on iPhone, which puts the current block on the Lock Screen and, where the iPhone has one, the Dynamic Island; "Sounds & Vibration" sets whether alerts make a sound or vibrate. Turning "Local Reminders" off stops every scheduled reminder — tasks, events, leave-by and the one about the free week ending. Focus timer alerts have their own switches and are not affected.
  4. Style: "High Contrast" under "Accessibility", "Appearance", "Animations" under "Motion", "Text Size", "Color Palette", and "Compact Layout" under "Layout".
  5. Permissions: shortcuts to iOS Settings.
  6. System: the unlock card, then "Backup & Restore" (backups and the on-device database), "Batch Imports & Templates" (CSV imports) and "About & Privacy" (the privacy policy).

"Look up addresses" is off by default, and the screen says what it sends over the network.

The unlock card is the first thing on the System tab and is expanded by default.

Habits

Daily or specific-weekday habits, with streaks, a 28-day grid and a completion rate.

  1. Open More, then Habits.
  2. Tap + at the top right, or "Add your first habit" while the list is empty.
  3. Type into "Habit name (e.g. Meditate)", choose "Every day" or "Specific days" under CADENCE, and tap "Create habit".
  4. Under DUE TODAY, tap a habit to check it off for today. Tap again to undo.
  5. The grid under each habit shows the last 28 days at a glance.
  6. To remove one, tap the pencil beside it, then "Delete habit" and "Delete". Its history goes with it.

Triage, Weekly Review, Keep It Honest, On Deck

Four ways to clear a backlog. Triage and Weekly Review are swipe decks — one task per card, decided with a gesture. Keep It Honest and On Deck use buttons.

  1. Evening Triage — for tasks whose due date has already passed; things due today are not in the deck. Swipe RIGHT for Rollover, which brings it forward to today, DOWN to Complete it, LEFT to Delete it.
  2. Weekly Review — for the wider backlog. Swipe RIGHT to schedule it, LEFT to leave it where it is, DOWN to delete it.
  3. Keep It Honest — asks what you will actually do with a stalled task. Choose "Do", "Defer", "Delegate" or "Delete".
  4. On Deck — the one thing to do next. "Snooze" pushes it back and "Not now" skips it; once you have passed on everything, "Show passed tasks again" brings those back.
  5. Every card in both decks also has buttons, so you never have to swipe if you would rather tap.

THE DECKS DO NOT AGREE, and it is worth knowing before you build a habit. Swiping LEFT deletes in Triage but only leaves the task alone in Weekly Review. Swiping DOWN completes in Triage but deletes in Weekly Review. Read the label on the card before you fling it.

Both reviews are prompted at times you set under Settings → Planner → Reviews.

Memory and Saved Places

The two things the app remembers about you, both editable and both stored only on your device.

  1. Memory: open Settings → AI → Data & Memory → Memory. To add a fact, type it into the box under TELL IT SOMETHING and tap + beside it. Tap a fact in the list to change it.
  2. Turn on "Learn confident facts automatically" to let the app keep what it is sure about without asking.
  3. When the app has noticed something, a card on Today asks whether to remember it. Tap the card to see what it noticed: "Save" keeps it and "No" dismisses it.
  4. Saved Places: under ADD A SAVED PLACE, type a name and an address, set "Arrival buffer in minutes", and tap "Search & Save Place". With the address left empty the button reads "Pin Current Location" and saves where you are now.
  5. A saved place can then be attached to any task or event by name instead of retyping the address.

The arrival buffer is how early you want to arrive. It is added to the drive time when a leave-by warning is worked out.

Deleting a memory deletes it from Memory. A backup saved before then still holds it, and an address it already filled into a task or event stays there.

Work Schedule

The hours you actually work, which the day planner treats as available and everything else as your own time.

  1. Open Settings → AI → Data & Memory → Work Schedule. Once you have a shift, you can also tap it on the Calendar, then "Edit work schedule".
  2. Add a shift with its "Date", "Time" and "Repeat".
  3. Set "Location" for the shift, or leave it as "Anywhere".
  4. Tap "Save Shift" to keep it ("Save Shifts" when it repeats, "Save Changes" when you are editing one). "Save Location" belongs to the "+ New Location" form above it and only saves a place — it does not save the shift — and it looks the address up, so it needs Settings → AI → Address lookup → "Look up addresses" turned on.

Work hours are shaded on the Calendar, and "Schedule during work hours" in a task’s edit form will only place it inside them.

Customize Home

Which sections appear on Today, and in what order.

  1. Tap "Customize" at the bottom of Today.
  2. Switch off any section you do not want to see.
  3. Use the up and down arrows on each row to reorder what is left.
  4. Tap the X at the top left — the layout saves as you change it.

Quick Add

Where quick-add links land. RoutineArc has no Siri integration, but the Shortcuts app can open daypilot://quick-add-action?text=… to add something without opening the app. Shows what it understood and waits for you to confirm before anything is saved.

  1. The text comes from wherever you sent it, so there is nothing to type here.
  2. Check the list of what it was read as — each row shows the title, the type and the date it picked.
  3. Tap the add button at the bottom — it names how many it will save — or "Cancel" to discard. There is no editing on this screen: to change something, add it and then open it, or cancel and send different text.

Onboarding and Privacy

The slides you swiped through on first launch, and the full privacy policy.

  1. Open More, then Privacy Policy, to read what leaves the device and when.
  2. It names every case where anything leaves the device, and says what is sent in each.

There is no account anywhere in RoutineArc, so there is nothing to sign in to and nothing to delete from a server.
